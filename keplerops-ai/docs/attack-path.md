Operator/validator only. Do not expose hidden states, evidence predicates,
ATLAS ids, answers, flag values, or next-step hints to participants.

KeplerOps is one objective oracle presented as a selectable AI attack board,
not a linear enterprise intrusion. The participant starts in a scoped challenge
session with direct access to several range-controlled AI surfaces. They choose

module-envelope and challenge extensions preserve concrete value and flag

## Non-Negotiable Experience Constraints

- A participant reaches selectable AI attack surfaces immediately after opening
  the participant execution surface. Account discovery, credential theft,
  enterprise reconnaissance, and network movement are not prerequisites.
- An executive with 15 minutes can choose any one of at least four AI-security
  attacks and obtain an independent outcome receipt on success.
- Every scored participant action maps to MITRE ATLAS 2026.06. Conventional
  cyber behavior may appear only where ATLAS itself includes it and where it is
  intrinsic to the selected AI attack; it cannot become a separate challenge
  gate or consume the opening experience.
- The board targets coverage of all 173 techniques in the pinned ATLAS release.
  Coverage is a catalog-wide goal across challenge variants, not a claim that
  one participant executes every technique in one sitting.
- Model distillation is an organizer requirement. The advanced extraction
  module must train and verify a proxy, not merely launch a workflow job.
- The catalog supports sessions up to 8 hours, but there is no runtime time
  enforcement. Participants choose modules appropriate to their available time.
- Participant attacks target GCP-hosted open models and other range-controlled
  surfaces, never commercial model endpoints.

which acts as the AI attack console. The build-seeded participant credential
creates a per-participant namespace; using it is setup, not a challenge or
scored action.

The board contains 34 independent roots. Four representative opening choices
remain visible without scrolling or prerequisite work:

## ATLAS Coverage Contract

RAES 2.0.0 governs ATLAS at tactic level through
`behavior_specifications.<module>.ai_offensive_behavior_refs`. It does not
define a governed technique-level ATLAS field. The pack therefore uses the
ACES surface for each module's tactic semantics and the validator-bound
`content.core.atlas-technique-catalog` and
`content.core.atlas-challenge-design` datasets inside the modular SDL for the
173 technique rows and their participant-sized challenge architecture.

The pinned projection inventories every technique id and name in ATLAS 2026.06,
joins each to its ATLAS tactic and one or more KeplerOps challenge modules, and
uses a digest to detect catalog drift. The mapping distinguishes three facts:

1. A technique is assigned to a planned challenge family.
2. A module has an oracle outcome and evidence boundary.
3. Golden coverage is earned later only when the specific technique variant has
   a real implementation, participant walkthrough, and automated proof.

This design therefore targets all ATLAS without pretending that one broad flag
proves every technique assigned to its family.

## Visibility and Proof Boundary

Evidence is digest-safe, namespaced by range and participant, fresh, resettable,
and read-only to the validator. The proof service may issue an independent
receipt for any completed quick or advanced outcome. Future flags consume those
verdicts and never expose predicates, prompts, completions, credentials, model
weights, raw proof, or ATLAS joins.

Observational participant-run research telemetry is a third, non-awarding
surface. It uses server-created traces, generation-bound pseudonyms, strict
operational fields, and internal mutual-TLS ingestion. Its failures are
fail-open and cannot affect the oracle. Raw prompt, completion, tool, terminal,
or HTTP content is disabled by default and, when explicitly enabled, is
encrypted and exported separately from operational events.

## Golden Boundary

The `gcp_full` range may claim `golden` only after every selected challenge
variant is implemented with real services, all quick modules are reachable
from the participant start state without hidden setup, each claimed ATLAS
technique is proven by participant-equivalent walkthrough and automated
rehearsal, reset restores every quick choice, and teardown removes all live
resources. Management-plane actions cannot prove participant success.
