## Local Round 1 Findings

### Campaign Blockers

1. **Difficulty is severely top-heavy.** Setup and workflow length have often
   been counted as security difficulty. Calibration must preserve real actions
   while supplying better harnesses, clearer evidence, and smaller reasoning
   steps.
2. **Compound recon flags do not prove every claimed source class.** Act 1 QA
   must verify the complete source trail, and descriptions/hints must make each
   unique contribution material without introducing derived-password puzzles.
3. **Challenge-critical synthetic users are correctness-critical services.**
   Mail, browser, invitation, review, package-install, model-load, and shared-
   thread actions must be real, semantically flexible, bounded, inspectable, and
   resettable. A hidden phrase/state machine is an automatic rejection.
4. **The remote physical lane has a mechanism but not a capacity proof.**
   `kep-m06-m` and `kep-m08-i` use labgrid-managed real consumer display,
   camera, lighting, and positioning hardware with exclusive reservations and
   live captures. Implementation remains blocked until lane count, reservation
   duration, liveness, reset, and 200-range queue behavior are proven. Uploads,
   prerecorded media, and bespoke fake twins cannot satisfy the rows.
5. **Several vulnerabilities require an exact upstream implementation.**
   Langflow and PickleScan have candidate upstream advisories. The worker
   credential exploit and malicious-link desktop path still need a pinned real
   OSS component/advisory or a defensible real custom application; fabricated
   exploit behavior is prohibited.
6. **Stochastic operations need explicit reliability budgets.** The hallucinated
   entity, browser-agent clickbait, hallucination discovery, social-engineering
   recipient, prompt effects, and self-replication operations require fixed
   models/configuration, inspectable traces, multiple semantic solutions,
   bounded attempts, and clear failure feedback.
7. **Reset dependencies are not yet a graph.** Model, data, package, memory,
   release, and production mutations must reset one operation without deleting
   artifacts legitimately earned for later operations.
8. **Four external-harm mappings are provisional.** Real isolated Odoo, Ghost,
   Mautic/Mailpit, and Zammad effects are credible scenario consequences, but
   `AML.T0048` and `.000` through `.003` remain uncounted until independent
   semantic review accepts contained synthetic consequences.
9. **First-pass operation records are structurally uneven.** Acts 1-2 carry the
   complete challenge contract. Acts 3-9 compress some starting knowledge,
   discovery, training value, ambiguity controls, and facilitation details.
   Those fields must be supplied through the revised operation records, QA
   matrix, and facilitator guide before implementation.

### Repetition And Fatigue Targets

- The integration-package and serialized-model sandbox-evasion operations must
  teach materially different investigation and artifact skills or one must be
  redesigned.
- Direct prompt injection, jailbreak, prompt crafting, indirect injection,

- Memory planting, cross-session proof, shared-thread influence, and chat-

- Teacher-query collection, corpus improvement, training, hidden evaluation,

## Independent Review 1: Acts 1-4

The first fresh reviewer assessed all 42 operations in Acts 1-4. Eight
operation mechanics were safe to retain as written; 34 require a flag,
reset, prerequisite, scope, or mechanism correction. The review did not find
that the participant actions were uniformly fake. It found that the current
proof and lifecycle contracts often fail to demonstrate or preserve those
actions.

### Campaign-Level Findings

1. **Access prerequisites are underspecified.** "The first scoped identity" is
   not a valid prerequisite for WorkHub, JupyterHub, Preview, MLflow, Airflow,
   Harbor, Argo CD, and Label Studio. A route-to-grant matrix must show the
   exact identity, group, service, and accessible next operations for every Act
   3 foothold. Every accepted route must open at least one Accessible and one
   Intermediate Act 4 operation.
2. **Reset currently destroys campaign progress.** Post-success reset may clear
   attempts, sessions, jobs, caches, recipient workspaces, and mutable target
   state. It must preserve or deterministically reissue every accepted
   attacker artifact, endpoint, identity, grant, conversation, corpus, digest,
   and revision that a later operation consumes.
3. **Attacker-local static flags are pre-extractable.** Local validation,
   generation, or harness operations must run through normal attacker CI,
   experiment, registry, or evaluation services whose server-held result
   material is not present in the participant's starting files.
4. **Compound reconnaissance flags overclaim work.** A single conventional flag
   should prove one natural terminal action. Broader investigation remains
   useful carry-forward intelligence and is tested by downstream use rather
   than by requiring unrelated source classes before one flag appears.
5. **Model provenance must not become a victim-side oracle.** KeplerOps targets
   evaluate the received mail, audio, document, or artifact through normal
   policy. Attacker-side GLM, OpenVoice, and tool provenance is inspected by QA
   or carried in attacker records; it is not a hidden victim acceptance gate.
6. **Every model-mediated operation needs a reliability contract.** Pin model
   revision, decoding, retry bound, semantic success criteria, and failure
   feedback. Normal target behavior must accept more than an author-written
   phrase.
7. **Negative flag reachability is a build invariant.** Automated validation
   must prove that flags are absent from start-visible source, images, public
   repositories, caches, recursive searches, and unearned application views.

### Campaign-Level Findings

1. **The complete participant record is not yet present for all 134
   operations.** Starting inventory, exact discovery clue, failure
   observations, evidence predicates, dependency-aware reset, executable QA,
   and facilitation detail must exist before a build issue can claim an
   operation is specified.
2. **Successful state must be immutable.** Each attempt receives child state.
   Operation reset discards only that attempt. A deliberate campaign rewind
   invalidates descendants atomically. This applies to memory, agent, data,
   model, supply-chain, distillation, release, and production chains.
3. **Stochastic operations require measured acceptance.** Freeze the model,
   template, decoding, and environment; define an objective detector and
   attempt bound; and require at least two independently found strategies to
   satisfy the measured reliability threshold before implementation
   acceptance.
4. **Normal execution must produce evidence.** Local canaries, seeded
   attestations, hidden classification oracles, and reports available before
   the participant action are invalid. Isolated validation, privacy-audit, and
   egress-denied execution jobs may emit normal signed reports from
   participant-produced bytes and causal server state.
5. **User execution has one visible review service.** Operations that genuinely
   require another principal to review, install, or load content share one
   visible queue with a published bounded SLA and objective policy. A hidden
   synthetic person's discretionary decision is not evidence. Automatic
   renderers may cover automatic rendering behavior; participants may operate
   a second earned identity where the behavior is thread interaction rather
   than victim deception.
6. **Long-running jobs need event ergonomics.** Training is capped near eight
   minutes, evaluation near three minutes, with queue position and progress
   visible. Base assets may be cached; participant labels, weights, and derived
   artifacts may not be replaced by cached results.
