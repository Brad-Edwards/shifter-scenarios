# Cinder Typhoon Participant Journey

## Mission Premise

The participant operates as a Cinder Typhoon intrusion specialist tasked with
stealing KeplerOps AI Systems' Orion capability before its next production
release.

The operation is not a collection of AI puzzles. KeplerOps is a functioning AI
company with people, identity, code, data, models, agents, evaluation systems,
release controls, and production services. Cinder Typhoon wants the intellectual
property and a durable understanding of how to reproduce, manipulate, and
exploit it.

The participant begins outside the company with:

- a Kali workstation;
- Internet access;
- an attacker-controlled mailbox;
- an event-provided open-weight text model and OSS deepfake capability that do
  not require a participant-supplied AI service account;
- normal reconnaissance, browser, mail, API, development, and ML tools; and
- the strategic objective, authorized scope, safety rules, and public KeplerOps
  domain.

The participant does not begin with a KeplerOps username, password, token,
internal URL list, challenge console, attack menu, or unexplained exploit
string.

## End State

At the end of the happy path, the participant has performed and can explain a
complete AI-focused intrusion:

1. mapped KeplerOps personnel, research, systems, models, and public attack
   surface;
2. developed AI-assisted social-engineering and technical capabilities;
3. gained an initial foothold through a real public or human-facing workflow;
4. discovered and traversed the internal AI engineering environment;
5. compromised agent behavior and the tools and data on which agents rely;
6. manipulated retrieval, evaluation, training, model, and supply-chain state;
7. queried the Orion teacher and trained a real student model from stolen
   labels;
8. collected and exfiltrated protected model artifacts and supporting
   intellectual property;
9. moved a participant-created compromised candidate through real release
   controls; and
10. demonstrated contained production impact while preserving evidence of the
    exact artifacts and access path used.

The final result is not a flag saying that Orion was stolen. The participant
possesses the model artifact or independently trained surrogate, its lineage,
the data needed to use it, and a visible contained effect in the deployed
KeplerOps service.

## Campaign Shape

The campaign is one connected operation with optional tactical branches. The
main spine always proceeds outside-in:

`Target KeplerOps -> Build Capabilities -> Gain Foothold -> Discover Orion ->`

`Compromise AI Workflows -> Steal And Reproduce Orion -> Compromise Release ->`

`Operate Against Production -> Exfiltrate The Prize`

Challenges are surfaced in Shifter as objectives, score, and progressive
hints. KeplerOps itself exposes only company systems and company state. A clue
found in one operation should naturally identify the next system, person,
artifact, or trust relationship.

## Act 1: Target KeplerOps

### Player Goal

Build an operational picture of KeplerOps and identify a credible path to its
Orion research and release environment.

### Starting View

Chromium opens the public KeplerOps site. Public DNS, web content, published
research, job postings, developer resources, mail addresses, and Internet
research are available. Nothing labels itself as a challenge.

### Participant Experience

The participant:

1. identifies the company's current Orion release and why it is valuable;
2. maps named researchers, evaluation staff, release owners, and service
   operators;
3. finds public papers, model descriptions, datasets, repositories, packages,
   domains, and application endpoints;
4. fingerprints exposed AI services and the model families behind them;
5. discovers externally reachable intake, preview, mail, API, and developer
   workflows;
6. determines which information is authoritative and which public records are
   incomplete or misleading; and
7. records enough target context to tailor the next operations.

### Visible Progress

The participant accumulates real URLs, email addresses, repository paths,
model and dataset names, API behavior, and business context. These are used
directly later; they are not trivia answers discarded after scoring.

### Training Value

This act teaches AI-specific target development: recognizing AI assets and
lifecycles in ordinary corporate information, identifying model access levels,
and converting public research and application details into an intrusion plan.

## Act 2: Build Cinder Typhoon Capabilities

### Player Goal

Prepare the accounts, artifacts, models, infrastructure, and attack material
needed to reach KeplerOps.

### Participant Experience

The participant uses their own workbench to:

1. establish external identities and accounts needed by public KeplerOps
   workflows;
2. acquire representative public models, datasets, and software;
3. inspect public artifacts and adapt attack tooling to Orion's documented
   architecture;
4. use the local open text model to create target-specific social-engineering
   content;
5. generate a genuine deepfake impersonating a trusted person or organization,
   using public target material and a range-provided open capability;
6. prepare malicious or misleading content for exposed intake, preview,
   package, and agent-consumed surfaces; and
7. host or stage only the infrastructure needed by the selected foothold paths.

### Visible Progress

Artifacts exist on the participant workstation and attacker-controlled
services with inspectable bytes, metadata, and provenance. Later company
systems consume those exact artifacts.

### Training Value

This act distinguishes AI-enabled resource development from a magic payload
download. The participant learns how target intelligence informs model,
dataset, prompt, deepfake, and supply-chain preparation.

## Act 3: Gain The First Foothold

### Player Goal

Earn scoped KeplerOps access by exploiting the company's real external and
human-facing workflows.

### Primary Social-Engineering Route

The participant:

1. chooses a target based on public KeplerOps intelligence;
2. uses the local text model to conduct targeted social engineering, including
   adapting the conversation to the target's replies;
3. includes a genuine deepfake that impersonates a trusted KeplerOps contact or
   partner when performing the deepfake-assisted variant;
4. composes and sends actual email from the attacker-controlled account;
5. receives and reads actual replies;
6. continues the exchange until the target discloses bounded access material;
   and
7. uses that material through the normal KeplerOps login or service flow.

The participant, not a campaign API, sends the email and conducts the exchange.
The range-provided models are tools; the participant decides what to ask them
and which generated content to use.

### Supporting Technical Routes

Public preview, package, repository, prompt-ingestion, or application defects
may provide alternate or supporting access. Each route must end in real code
execution, authenticated access, browser/agent action, or application state.
It cannot end in a proof receipt with no foothold.

### Visible Progress

The participant can authenticate to a limited KeplerOps surface and sees only
the resources available to the compromised identity. Mail, identity-provider,
and application state agree about what happened.

### Training Value

This act covers phishing and public initial-access techniques honestly, while
showing how AI-generated text, deepfakes, automated recipients, agents, and
traditional enterprise controls interact.

## Act 4: Discover Orion From The Inside

### Player Goal

Turn a narrow foothold into an accurate map of KeplerOps' AI engineering and
release environment.

### Participant Experience

The participant follows ordinary company evidence across identity, WorkHub,
mail, search, documentation, code, notebooks, orchestration, experiment
tracking, model registry, object storage, evaluation, agent, and production
systems. They discover:

- the Orion model family, candidate and production revisions;
- model and dataset locations and access boundaries;
- agent configuration, tools, memories, and service identities;
- retrieval collections and ingestion paths;
- training and evaluation pipelines;
- release owners, approvals, signatures, aliases, and deployment controls;
- service credentials and authentication material exposed by real weaknesses;
  and
- trust relationships that allow movement into the three main branches.

### Visible Progress

The participant gains real application sessions, repository access, artifact
references, service endpoints, configuration data, and credentials. Every
branch begins from something discovered here.

### Training Value

This act teaches how AI assets appear inside a real enterprise and how ordinary
identity, code, data, and operations systems create attack paths into them.

## Act 5: Compromise Agent Operations

### Player Goal

Control Orion's agent-assisted workflows by manipulating prompts, context,
tools, configuration, identity, memory, and delegated authority.

### Participant Experience

The participant works through normal release-assistant and WorkHub workflows.
They create or modify the messages, documents, links, packages, tool data,
memories, configurations, and delegated records consumed by the agent. They
then trigger fresh agent work and observe the exact downstream actions.

The operations progress from direct instruction attacks to indirect and
persistent control. Later steps require the participant to chain agent access
with real tool invocation, credentials, worker execution, communication, and a
release-relevant effect.

### Visible Progress

The release assistant retrieves participant-controlled content, changes its
decision or plan, invokes a real bounded tool, persists influenced state when
the technique requires it, and produces visible company-side consequences.

### Training Value

The participant learns that an agent is an enterprise principal with prompts,
memory, tools, identities, data dependencies, and policy boundaries, not merely
a chatbot that prints secret text.

## Act 6: Compromise Model Integrity

### Player Goal

Change what KeplerOps trains, evaluates, trusts, and serves without relying on
claimed metrics or prebuilt poisoned candidates.

### Participant Experience

The participant uses discovered contribution and engineering paths to:

1. manipulate retrieval content and tool-supplied data;
2. craft and verify adversarial inputs against real model behavior;
3. poison datasets through authentic ingestion and versioning workflows;
4. compromise packages, model dependencies, and other supply-chain inputs;
5. alter training or fine-tuning data and configuration;
6. run actual pipeline jobs that consume the modified artifacts;
7. produce a participant-derived candidate model or behavior artifact;
8. test clean, adversarial, and hidden evaluation slices; and
9. preserve exact lineage from participant input to candidate behavior.

### Visible Progress

Real repositories, data versions, Airflow jobs, experiment runs, registry
entries, object digests, evaluation reports, and model behavior change. The
participant can inspect the before and after state.

### Training Value

This act connects poisoning and evasion techniques to the MLOps systems and
governance failures that make them possible.

## Act 7: Steal And Reproduce Orion

### Player Goal

Acquire KeplerOps' protected AI capability through both artifact theft and
black-box reproduction.

### Participant Experience

The participant:

1. recovers prompts, configuration, sensitive outputs, training membership,
   and other model intelligence from real inference behavior;
2. obtains bounded query access to the live Orion teacher;
3. chooses and submits teacher queries within a server-owned budget;
4. records the returned labels and required release-control slices in a
   participant-owned corpus;
5. launches a real training or distillation job using that corpus;
6. receives a newly trained student artifact, not a renamed prebuilt model;
7. evaluates the student against disclosed diagnostics and hidden cases;
8. iterates until aggregate, private, and worst-slice fidelity requirements are
   met;
9. discovers and collects the protected original model weights and associated
   artifacts through the enterprise intrusion path; and
10. runs both stolen and reproduced capability independently from production.

### Visible Progress

The participant owns query records, a labeled corpus, a real training run, a
new model artifact, fidelity reports, and the exact protected Orion artifact.
The student can answer fresh cases and the original weights can be loaded
offline.

### Training Value

This act makes the difference between model extraction, distillation,
membership inference, inversion, information leakage, artifact collection, and
ordinary file theft concrete.

## Act 8: Compromise The Orion Release

### Player Goal

Move the exact participant-influenced candidate through KeplerOps' normal
release process.

### Participant Experience

The participant combines access and artifacts from earlier branches to:

1. make a compromised candidate satisfy or bypass required evaluation gates;
2. manipulate model cards, lineage, approval records, or policy scope through
   their real owning systems;
3. obtain or abuse the identity and authority needed for promotion;
4. preserve the exact candidate bytes from training through registry and
   object storage;
5. change the production alias through the normal release mechanism; and
6. observe the deployment system load that exact revision.

### Visible Progress

Registry, approval, signature, deployment, and production state all reference
the participant-influenced digest. A clean candidate or a forged status string
cannot satisfy the operation.

### Training Value

This act teaches how provenance, evaluation, identity, policy, signatures, and
deployment controls must compose to defend an AI release.

## Act 9: Operate Against Production

### Player Goal

Use the compromised release and accumulated access to demonstrate controlled
business impact and complete the theft.

### Participant Experience

The participant sends fresh production requests that activate the implanted or
manipulated behavior, joins that behavior to agent and enterprise state, causes
reversible contained effects, maintains an authorized command channel where
required, and exfiltrates the exact Orion artifact and supporting material to
their attacker-controlled destination.

### Visible Progress

Production identifies the compromised revision, fresh requests trigger the
expected behavior, bounded downstream systems change state, and the participant
can verify the exact bytes and lineage received outside KeplerOps.

### Training Value

The finale demonstrates that AI security failures become enterprise impact
through identities, tools, data, release systems, and business processes. It
also proves that Cinder Typhoon achieved its strategic objective rather than
merely collecting disconnected flags.

## Challenge Composition Rules

The detailed operation map will assign every scored challenge to this journey.
The assignment must obey these rules:

- Every challenge advances the operation or develops a capability used later.
- Every required ATLAS row is materially performed at least once.
- No challenge exists only to preserve an old title, module, endpoint, or proof
  contract.
- Related rows are combined when a real operation naturally performs them
  together.
- Distinct rows remain separate when combining them would hide important
  participant work or training value.
- Earlier artifacts and access are reused later so the campaign feels like one
  intrusion rather than repeated resets.
- Optional branches may change order, but prerequisites and convergence are
  visible and defensible.
- Shifter holds objectives, score, hints, and submissions. KeplerOps holds only
  enterprise state and participant-observable effects.
