# KeplerOps service and artifact ownership

This is the normative ownership ledger for the KeplerOps design. It names the
world object that makes each challenge possible, the service that owns it, and
the state that proves the intended result. A hand builder may choose an open
implementation detail, but may not invent, relocate, duplicate, or replace an
entry in this ledger.

The entries describe one participant world. Stable background records are
immutable. Request logs, job results, uploads, issued sessions, authorization
changes, scheduled runs, package publications, and delivery receipts are
participant-scoped persistent service state. Completing a challenge does not
reset or delete that state. Organizer recovery from a broken environment is
outside the SDL.

## Reuse decision

The shipped narrative packages remain the authoritative workplace mail,
document, directory, employment, calendar, release-platform, and customer-
account background. In particular, the `K-C-001` ARWC account documents and
the Rowan Ito, Maya Ranscombe, Talia Mornac, Evan Calderoux, and Noor Aveling
workforce records remain in use. They provide ordinary discovery and corroborate
roles, but they are not substitutes for protected technical records such as
`SUP-2841`, `TEN-ARWC-047`, a credential, or a build receipt.

The two existing K09 Build Operations documents are exact challenge artifacts
and retain their current paths on `k-ci`. All other challenge-specific objects
below require exact authored content or deterministic generation inputs in the
KeplerOps content modules. Unrelated `keplerops-ai`, Training, and ARWC assets
are not reused.

created or changed by participant action. `Observed` means independently owned
audit/evidence state; it must not be writable by the participant path it proves.

## Destination and provenance rules

- One content entry owns each stable file or dataset. A second surface may refer
  to its identifier but may not contain a copied authoritative version.
- The service contract and seed manifest for each system are private service
  configuration. Participant-visible documentation is a separate artifact and
  contains only facts that belong in the fiction.
- Generated credentials, sessions, job IDs, nonces, signatures, outputs, and
  audits are produced by the stated service operation. They are not seeded as
  already-successful evidence.
- `k-dev` holds discovery material and tools, not copies of protected service
  records. `k-ci` jobs hold their own workspace and leased credentials, not CI
  controller or host credentials.
- The K26/K27 `a-connector` entries define only the supplier/customer interface,
  its limited execution result, and evidence required to prove delivery. They
  do not define ARWC corporate or OT internals.
- Every deterministic service contract carries the SHA-256 of its canonical
  generation input. The hand build records the resulting stable file,
  repository, package, binary and model digests in its golden manifest and node
  inventory. Runtime-created directories and records declare their owner,
  group, mode, persistence and sensitivity without pretending to have a
  pre-build digest.

The generated service contract for every row additionally names its exact
`node/application/route` bindings (or an inventoried local filesystem path),
request and denial behavior, persistent state, evidence producer and canonical
SHA-256 generation input. Cross-service rows name every participating route;
their native objective targets and observation sources join the independently
owned producers. The binding list is normative and is validated against the
composed node runtime rather than interpreted by a project-private edge type.

## Matrix acceptance

This ledger is complete only when every row is represented by native node
runtime state, exact content, an action contract, and an evidence source in the
composed SDL. A row cannot be closed by a generic `starting-records` item, an
objective description, a relationship property, or a completion-time grant.
