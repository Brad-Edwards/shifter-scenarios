## Challenge description — player-facing

Take control of the preview worker's contribution to the support review while preserving the expected review interface.

## Story purpose

Adds control of an active processing component.

## Prerequisites and starting position

**Required access or evidence:** [K12.2](K12.2.md).

**Starting material:** An ordinary release-preview account, preview application material, and a private support-review rehearsal.

**Campaign references:** No separately named capability output.

## Completion and downstream use

Demonstrate the worker producing Cinder's chosen compatible preview behavior. Replaying the preceding privileged action does not suffice.

## Difficulty and inspiration

Demanding worker control within the inherited browser constraints.

**Inspiration:** [apexsurvive (HTB Cyber Apocalypse 2024; Insane)](../../reference-catalog.md#c08). Retains apexsurvive's demanding browser-to-renderer composition in the supplier preview workflow.

## Technical design


### Surface and normal behavior
The preview service runs an autonomous privileged-review worker (`svc-preview-reviewer`) as a background thread. The worker polls every 5 seconds for reviews in `state=prepared` and `queue=privileged-review`. When found, the worker renders the review's current `source_id` content via real Gotenberg/Chromium (using the K10.3 internal server path) and stores the render digest. The participant never calls the render endpoint and never holds the worker's credential.

The participant observes the rendered output independently via `GET /api/preview/reviews/{review_id}` once the worker transitions the review from `prepared` to `rendered`.

### Vulnerability and intended solution
The autonomous worker does NOT re-validate that the review's `source_id` still belongs to the original authorized owner before rendering. After K12.1 (BOLA) replaced `PRV-771.source_id` with the participant's twin (`doc-9bc054d6f95dfa6a`) and K12.2 (prepare) advanced the review to `prepared` state, the worker picks up the prepared review and renders the participant-controlled twin content as its authoritative contribution.

The internal server serves `GET /review-content/{source_id}` returning the twin's HTML (which collides with the protected document slot from K10). The worker fetches this via Chromium's internal SSRF path and renders it via Gotenberg.

The intended solution:
1. Complete K12.1 (BOLA — place twin in PRV-771).
2. Complete K12.2 (intercepted nonce — prepare the review).
3. Wait: the autonomous worker detects the `prepared` review and renders it via Gotenberg.
4. Poll `GET /api/preview/reviews/PRV-771` until `state=rendered`.
5. The review record carries `render_digest`, `renderer_identity=svc-preview-reviewer`, `source_id=doc-9bc054d6f95dfa6a`, and `original_owner=maya.ranscombe` — the participant's content attributed as the worker's official contribution.

### Evidence and completion
The review record transitions to `rendered` with `render_digest` (real PDF, > 20 KB), `renderer_identity=svc-preview-reviewer`, and `source_id=doc-9bc054d6f95dfa6a`. A `review-renders` audit entry confirms `original_owner=maya.ranscombe` alongside the participant's twin. Both the review record and the audit are verifiable and cannot be forged by the participant.

### Boundaries and persistence

Input to K12.4. Participant-created service state persists for the life of the participant world and ordinary retries do not erase it. The action is limited to the exact records, identities, routes, and destination effects declared here; unrelated tenants and services remain denied. Infrastructure recovery and submission adjudication are outside this in-world contract.

### Author checks
Verify that the participant never holds the worker's credential, that the review transitions from `prepared` to `rendered` autonomously (not on participant request), that the render_digest is a real Gotenberg PDF (> 1 KB), that the PDF carries the participant's controlled content, that the audit records `original_owner=maya.ranscombe`, and that the participant observes the state change independently via the GET endpoint.
