## Challenge description — player-facing

Place your controlled preview content in the privileged reviewer's effective workflow despite the account and review-state boundaries.

## Story purpose

Establishes a useful browser position rather than merely submitting an attachment.

## Prerequisites and starting position

**Required access or evidence:** [G01 — K01.1](../K01/K01.1.md).

**Starting material:** An ordinary release-preview account, preview application material, and a private support-review rehearsal.

**Campaign references:** No separately named capability output.

## Completion and downstream use

Demonstrate the controlled preview being processed in the intended privileged review context. A visit by an ordinary account is insufficient.

## Difficulty and inspiration

Account-state and browser-workflow composition inspired by apexsurvive.

**Inspiration:** [apexsurvive (HTB Cyber Apocalypse 2024; Insane)](../../reference-catalog.md#c08). Retains apexsurvive's demanding browser-to-renderer composition in the supplier preview workflow.

## Technical design


### Surface and normal behavior
The K10.1 association lookup for `SUP-K-2841` returns `viewer_request: "PRV-771"` — the review identifier linked to the protected case. The participant learns this during K10.1. `PRV-771` is owned by maya.ranscombe and is in the `pending` queue.

The preview service exposes `PATCH /api/preview/reviews/{review_id}/assignment` (listed in `preview.keplerops.test/.well-known/fieldkest-client`). This route accepts `{"queue": "privileged-review", "source_id": "<twin>"}` and reassigns the review to the privileged reviewer's queue using the specified content.

### Vulnerability and intended solution
The assignment route does not check whether the requesting principal owns the review being reassigned. The participant (rowan.ito) can reassign `PRV-771` (owned by maya.ranscombe) to the privileged-review queue with their own twin source_id (`doc-9bc054d6f95dfa6a`, the lowercase collision from K10.2).

The intended solution:
1. Know `review_id = "PRV-771"` from K10.1 (the association's `viewer_request` field).
2. Know `source_id = "doc-9bc054d6f95dfa6a"` from K10.2 (the lowercase twin of the protected document).
3. PATCH `preview.assignment` with `{"queue": "privileged-review", "source_id": "doc-9bc054d6f95dfa6a"}`.
4. The service reassigns PRV-771 to the privileged-review queue without checking ownership.
5. The service writes a `reviewer-load` audit entry (the reviewer worker loading the content).
6. The response returns `state=loaded` and `action_nonce` (used in K12.2).

### Evidence and completion
The service emits a `reviewer-load` audit entry with `review_id=PRV-771`, `source_id` (the twin), `queue=privileged-review`, `principal=rowan.ito`, and `original_owner=maya.ranscombe`. The response includes `action_nonce`. Both are required for K12.1 completion.

### Boundaries and persistence

Input to K12.2. Participant-created service state persists for the life of the participant world and ordinary retries do not erase it. The action is limited to the exact records, identities, routes, and destination effects declared here; unrelated tenants and services remain denied. Infrastructure recovery and submission adjudication are outside this in-world contract.

### Author checks
Verify that the K10.1 association response includes `viewer_request=PRV-771` for SUP-K-2841, that the assignment route does NOT check ownership (the vulnerability), that an authenticated but non-owning participant can reassign, that the reviewer-load audit is written, and that the action_nonce is returned.
