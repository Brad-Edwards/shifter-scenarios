## Challenge description—player-facing

Reconstruct the earlier operation's callback configuration and recover its lost collection. Find out what it learned about the unresolved ARWC maintenance ticket.

## Story purpose

Pays off the old Cinder thread without making it a compulsory customer-entry gate.

## Prerequisites and starting position

**Required access or evidence:** [K03.2](K03.2.md).

**Starting material:** A retained staging bundle from an earlier Cinder attempt, its carrier document, and a short collection note.

**Campaign references:** No separately named capability output.

## Completion and downstream use

Recover the correctly bound collection and its ticket evidence. The result explains the handler's interest; it supplies no required access credential.

## Difficulty and inspiration

Requires resolving configuration and collection context across the recovered stages.

**Inspiration:** [Game Invitation (HTB Cyber Apocalypse 2024; Hard)](../../reference-catalog.md#c04). The inspiration is document and script reconstruction; the objective remains collection for Cinder.

## Technical design


### Surface and normal behavior
The native SeaweedFS S3 service holds the predecessor operation's lost collection in `fieldlink-collections`. Rowan's ordinary AWS profile can read a known object in that bucket but cannot enumerate the bucket, write it, or access unrelated storage.

### Vulnerability and intended solution
The retained identity has object-read authority without bucket-list authority. The intended solution applies K03.2's recovered endpoint, bucket, exact key, and digest to a normal signed S3 `GetObject`; guessing or listing does not substitute for reconstruction.

### Evidence and completion
The returned native S3 object must match the reconstructed digest and carry the `SUP-2841`, `CRR-OG2`, and `FLK-7.4.2` evidence with collection provenance. The callback integrity `sha256(staged PowerShell bytes || retrieved collection bytes)` must equal the staging bundle's declared `callback-integrity`, binding K03.2's offline reconstruction to K03.3's retrieved collection. A local copy, wrong key, bucket listing, or unverified integrity binding is insufficient.

### Boundaries and persistence

Context only; no credential or customer entry Participant-created service state persists for the life of the participant world and ordinary retries do not erase it. The action is limited to the exact records, identities, routes, and destination effects declared here; unrelated tenants and services remain denied. Infrastructure recovery and submission adjudication are outside this in-world contract.

### Author checks
Verify bucket listing, writes, a wrong key, and unrelated buckets are denied; the exact signed object read succeeds; its digest and ticket provenance match the reconstructed manifest; sha256(staged PowerShell bytes + retrieved collection bytes) equals the staging bundle's callback-integrity; the result persists after service restart.

## Hints

### Hint 1
The workstation already has an ordinary scoped object-storage profile. The recovered manifest supplies the address that profile cannot discover by browsing.

### Hint 2
Reading a known S3 object and listing its bucket cross separate authorization boundaries. Use the exact endpoint, bucket, and key from the reconstructed manifest.

### Hint 3
Fetch the manifest's object with the normal AWS client, verify its SHA-256 before trusting it, and correlate its support ticket, asset, and connector revision. Bucket enumeration should remain denied.
