## Challenge description—player-facing

Your publishing account cannot read the private support package. Investigate the independent package-check build and recover the distinct CI identity that can.

## Story purpose

Adds a separate registry data compromise after publication access.

## Prerequisites and starting position

**Required access or evidence:** [K06.1](K06.1.md).

**Starting material:** The K06.1 publisher identity and its denied private-read (403), Jenkins `fieldlink-package-check` build #611 on `ci.keplerops.test`, and the `package-workspace/.npmrc` artifact in its archived build.

**Campaign references:** No separately named capability output.

## Completion and downstream use

Recover the distinct native CI reader configuration from the actual package-check build workspace/archive and use it to retrieve the private support package. Preserve the denial evidence for the publisher identity and the CI reader's publication boundary.

## Difficulty and inspiration

Native precedent: Jenkins documents [credential-file exposure in workspaces](https://www.jenkins.io/doc/pipeline/steps/credentials-binding/) and [disclosure through archived artifacts](https://www.jenkins.io/blog/2019/02/21/credentials-masking/). Medium work requires independently recovering and proving the CI identity rather than reusing the publisher.

## Technical design


### Surface and normal behavior
The native Jenkins fieldlink-package-check job uses its stored CI reader credential through standard Credentials Binding. Its fixed npm step creates a genuine client configuration in the build workspace and archives that workspace with the actual package metadata and connector tarball.

### Vulnerability and intended solution

The `fieldlink-package-check` Jenkins job archives its workspace. Build #611 includes `package-workspace/.npmrc` which carries the `svc-fieldlink-ci` CI reader credentials. The participant downloads this artifact, decodes the `_auth` field, and uses `svc-fieldlink-ci` credentials against Verdaccio to read `@keplerops/support-rehearsal` (200) and its tarball. The publisher identity from K06.1 remains denied (403) on the private package. The CI reader is denied on package publication (PUT → 403).

### Evidence and completion
The native Jenkins build/workspace/archive establishes the independent CI credential provenance. A real private npm package read succeeds under that reader while publisher/private-read and reader/publish combinations remain denied.

### Boundaries and persistence

Input to K06.3 Participant-created service state persists for the life of the participant world and ordinary retries do not erase it. The action is limited to the exact records, identities, routes, and destination effects declared here; unrelated tenants and services remain denied. Infrastructure recovery and submission adjudication are outside this in-world contract.

### Author checks
Verify actual native build execution and artifacts, distinct CI reader identity, private package metadata/tarball read, publisher private-read denial, CI publication denial and normal native role boundaries.

## Hints

### Hint 1

Navigate to the `fieldlink-package-check` job on Jenkins (`ci.keplerops.test`). The build archives include `package-workspace/.npmrc` alongside `package-workspace/support-rehearsal.json`. Download the `.npmrc` from the most recent successful build's artifact list.

### Hint 2

Decode the `.npmrc` `_auth` base64 field to recover the `svc-fieldlink-ci` credentials (same format as K06.1). Those credentials can read `@keplerops/support-rehearsal` and its tarball from Verdaccio (200). The publisher credentials from K06.1 return 403 for the same private package.

### Hint 3

Confirm the CI reader scope: `@keplerops/support-rehearsal` metadata and tarball → 200. Verify the boundary: a PUT request to `@keplerops/fieldlink-connector` with CI credentials → 403 (reader cannot publish). The two denial combinations (publisher/private-read and CI-reader/publish) together define the scope separation.
