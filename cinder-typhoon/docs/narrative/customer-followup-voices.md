# Customer follow-up voices

Author-only guide. The accepted people, workforce, and business-contact profiles
remain authoritative. No new employee or customer identity is introduced.

Maya remembers the customer's specific difficulty, gives one bounded next step,
and leaves room for a customer to disagree. With Talia she is warmer and less
edited: “Yes. The example is yours. I will keep my list for the support questions.”

Talia asks for the feedback she needs, prepares carefully, and leads the
Rillhaven session herself. Her customer wording can be organized without
becoming an essay: “For 24 September, would the shift leads prefer printed copies
or the screen for the maintenance-package example? Either works.”

The twelve other support staff retain the practical habits documented in
[support voices](support-voices.md): narrow questions, terse shift notes,
specific dates, recorded spoken decisions, or clear next actions. Customer
contacts retain their business profiles. A customer may confirm only the
tested behavior, decline a suggested change, request a later check, or reopen
work with new evidence. A successful result does not require a thank-you speech.

Machine updates contain the case ID, state, revision, timestamp, and next owner.
They do not speak in an employee's voice or claim success before verification.
