# Purchasing, stores, and supplier voices

Author-only guidance for recurring issue 126 speakers. Sample IDs refer to
`assets/narrative/authoring/mail-purchasing-stores.yaml`. These notes and excerpts
are not deployed to the workplace.

| Person | Working concern and writing habit | Finished sample |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Priya Naravel, district procurement officer | Keeps supplier scope, cost centre, and written authority together. Polished, direct, and sparing with greetings. | `ps-0001-order`: “This is the complete authority. A substitution needs written agreement before dispatch.” |
| Celia Maraven, procurement officer | Asks or answers one concrete question at a time; corrections are explicit rather than silently edited. | `ps-0002-order`: “Please book this against the stated order and receiving location.” |
| Cian Telmire, procurement officer | Uses terse reference-led notes and preserves exceptions in the written record. | `ps-0003-order`: “Approved as listed.” |
| Corin Darsell, procurement officer | Notices when substitutions or delivery changes create work for the receiving team. | `ps-0004-order`: “The cost-centre allocation is on the order.” |
| Dana Marrent, procurement officer | Records the decision after practical discussion and avoids reopening a settled scope in email. | `ps-0005-order`: “The order carries only the approved lines.” |
| Accounts officers | Name the record they matched and state whether it is payable or settled. | Hester Vale, Larkspur records-materials coordinator | Exact about archive box, folder, and label formats; calm about split practicalities. | `ps-0445-quote`: “Box, folder and label formats are shown separately so the records room can check them.” |

Requesters and receivers use their established departmental profiles. Their
purchase notes remain short because the requisition carries the authoritative
quantity, location, work reference, and cost centre. A supplier's polished PDF
does not make every accompanying message equally formal.
