# The story already in motion

This document describes authored history and narrative consequences. It does
not add challenges, solve steps, access gates, automatic timers, or a live
simulation. The established dependency graph remains authoritative.

## Before Cinder

ARWC bought FieldKest because it needed a dependable way to move field and
maintenance records between organizations that already had their own systems.
The early integration was useful. KeplerOps learned the customer's conventions;
ARWC acquired a supplier that answered difficult questions rather than asking
it to replace everything.

Later modernization affected several different things. ARWC moved its customer
integration to a new tenant record. KeplerOps retained an older supported
release and its history. Engineering commissioned a revised representation of
the outlet group. Field work produced a calibration change. Parts of the
business reporting path continued to use retained aliases and interpretations.

Those changes were not one project with one definition of completion. The
integration migration could be delivered while an engineering acceptance
question remained open. A historical record could still be a useful explanation
without being today's operational authority. The unresolved question passed
between people who each understood a different part of it.

The recurring support case began as a request for a diagnostic associated with
a routine outlet inspection. It kept returning because the successful package
and reassuring report did not settle the disagreement about current evidence
and reserve. A restricted escalation explains more than the ordinary scheduling
conversation does. Its contents remain challenge material, not a paragraph to
copy into every employee's inbox.

Now the dry-period allocation review gives the question a deadline in the
companies' working lives. ARWC needs a defensible recommendation. KeplerOps
needs an honest account of the handover it has delivered. This is the week
Cinder has chosen to act.

## What would happen if nobody attacked

The legitimate path is repairable. Theo obtains the required inspection
arrangements. Clara and the field team establish the technical evidence.
KeplerOps supports a handover whose delivered behaviour can be demonstrated.
Nadia reconciles the reserve position and qualifies anything still uncertain.
Priya retains or purchases replacement capacity only through the normal
decision process. Luc explains the cost of caution at the review.

The likely result is an inconvenient week, a more conservative allocation,
some scheduled work, and a difficult but salvageable supplier account review.
There is no inevitable disaster for Cinder merely to witness. The player's
actions create additional effects that can be distinguished from this baseline.

## Cinder's story

Cinder Typhoon is a fictional intrusion group with a bounded economic objective:
turn supplier access into the ability to impose a loss on ARWC during a
sensitive allocation period. No real state affiliation or larger geopolitical
conspiracy is needed. Participants are operators causing the fictional loss;
the story does not recast them as invited maintenance consultants.

Training gives operators a place to learn how to establish a useful result.
The developer foothold is already supplied through the earlier fictional
package-install compromise. Optional predecessor material can explain why the
group was interested in the supplier, but must not contain a missing compulsory
instruction.

The handler gives an objective and recognizes established consequences. It
does not explain every clue, supply unauthorized shortcuts, or know which
optional branch a participant has completed without that participant's state
supporting the callback. The enterprises tell most of the story through their
own retained work.

## Depth without a second hidden plot

Optional release work shows what it means to reproduce a supplier capability
through a projected rollover while preserving a different customer's behaviour.
Rillhaven gives that unaffected reference customer a recognizable business
identity. It does not become another company to attack.

Historical recovery can explain how a tenant migration and a calibration change
left two representations of the same plant. The ordinary routes still supply
all required current knowledge. Someone who completes the advanced history
gets a fuller explanation, not a missing paragraph without which the finale
makes no sense.

The more demanding process and reporting episodes deepen the same question:
what can be made to happen, what will a particular consumer believe happened,
and what financial decision follows? Their rehearsals keep separate state.
They do not escalate the fiction into a larger catastrophe or silently rewrite
a completed main ending.

## How the ending feels

The strongest callback is the change in what familiar people need to do.
Maya's ordinary handover has become relevant to a customer incident. Theo's
inspection planning now sits beside an unplanned operational consequence.
Clara's distinction between acknowledgement and observed behaviour has become
concrete. Nadia must revise the allocation recommendation. Priya must account
for a purchase that was previously only a contingency. Luc needs a continuity
statement he can stand behind.

Those reactions must follow the participant's actual established outcome.
Do not pre-author an always-visible inbox full of accusations or a triumphant
postmortem. Do not claim the attacker took a particular route if either ordinary
route could have produced the result. The eventual consequence content can
show what the companies observe without instantly identifying Cinder or
inventing a forensic investigation the scenario has not modelled.

The final effect is memorable because the organization has already become
familiar. It costs money, limits choices, and changes tomorrow's work for people
the player has encountered. It needs no victory popup inside an employee's
mailbox and no larger disaster to make it count.
