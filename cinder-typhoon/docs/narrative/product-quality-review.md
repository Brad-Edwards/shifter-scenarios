# FieldKest product and quality content review: issue 116

Author-only record. Opening state: 08:30 on 16 September 2026, UTC−04:00.

## Fit review before authoring

This slice starts from the accepted support and FieldKest engineering histories.
It uses the eight established Product management and quality staff, the five
existing repositories, the exact engineering revisions, and the thirty stable
support-to-engineering joins. It does not alter product architecture, challenge
logic, service identities, routes, credentials, or release state.

The initial adversarial pass identified six risks and established the source
rules used by the finished records:

1. Product acceptance could retroactively legitimize rejected engineering work.
   The accepted set is therefore the first 220 ordinary work items whose upstream
   state is `completed` or `accepted`; no rejected or deferred revision enters it.
2. The thirty engineering assessments could be misrepresented as fixes. They
   remain twenty deferred and ten declined intake decisions, with no test result,
   accepted version, release date, or customer-outcome claim.
3. A final roadmap could erase the history of changed priorities. Three retained
   revisions distinguish a proposal, a capacity revision, and the accepted
   evidence baseline. Proposed windows never become release commitments.
4. Tests could describe convenient behaviour that the engineering history never
   supplied. Every criterion, compatibility row, plan, result, and discussion
   names the exact upstream work item, repository, revision, topic, and version.
5. A sea of passing checks could look fabricated. Twenty-four accepted records
   preserve an initial mismatch and correction; forty pass with a stated
   limitation. The remaining results are bounded to named fixtures rather than
   claiming general correctness.
6. Repeated review voices could flatten eight people into one narrator. The mail
   retains each person's workforce writing habit, varies topic, repository,
   rationale, timing, and response rhythm, and includes direct questions,
   corrections, fragments, and decisions that do not resolve every disagreement.

Each decision has one sixteen-message reply chain. The evidence set contains a
decision, research note, acceptance criteria, CSV compatibility workbook, test
plan, meeting minutes, and documentation review. Accepted records also contain
a focused test result. The six shared records are three roadmap revisions, an
acceptance register, a limitations register, and the review calendar.

Decision rationales are deliberately different. Reusable behaviour and broad
customer usefulness can justify acceptance; a useful request can remain deferred
when capacity or evidence is insufficient; a customer concern can be valid while
its proposed shortcut is declined as bespoke or too costly to compatibility.
Neither support nor product management is made foolish to create contrast.

## Finished-content checks

The completed records were read by decision state and upstream repository. The
review corrected these draft problems before acceptance:

- Early wording called every roadmap window “planned.” The finished decision
  and register fields call it proposed, while the commitment line records only
  evidence acceptance and leaves release scheduling downstream.
- A draft compatibility sheet used empty cells for unexecuted assessments. The
  shipped CSVs use explicit `NOT_RUN`, zero fixture counts, and the reason no
  acceptance was authorized.
- Initial meeting notes for declined requests sounded like engineering failures.
  They now preserve the customer concern and distinguish product reuse from the
  narrower requested shortcut.
- Some first-pass results said only “pass.” The accepted records now preserve
  expected and observed values, exact versions, fixture counts, correction
  history, and any remaining limitation.
- The first audience set was department-wide. Per-record material is now limited
  to the eight product/quality staff plus the named engineer, reviewer, release
  owner, and—only for a joined escalation—the support owner.

The visible-content scan rejects environment names, issue numbers, ownership
labels, authoring terminology, and challenge language. The source remains
ordinary reusable workplace content and the scenario overlay is empty.

## Delivery evidence and limits

The renderer regenerates the RFC 5322 mail, exact attachment bytes, source
digests, mailbox membership, document readers, artifact catalog, coverage map,
and existing default-open native SDL modules. Integrity checks verify all 250
engineering joins, thirty support joins, chronology, decisions, versions,
workbook values, attachments, audiences, retained copies, and limitation rows.

The remaining limitations are intentional. Product acceptance is not proof of
deployment, release, or customer notification. Deferred and declined assessments
do not close their support cases. Static authoring checks do not claim a running
workplace service or runtime readback.
