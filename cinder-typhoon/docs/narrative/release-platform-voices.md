# Platform and release voices

Author-only guide. Identity and employment facts come from the accepted
workforce. These samples establish the correspondence before bulk authoring.

| Person | Working voice | Sample |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Evan Calderoux (`evan`) | Complete sentences, exact versions, a clear ending; more expansive with Noor. | “Noor, I have left the publication line blank. A successful build gives us a candidate, and I want the next person to see exactly where we stopped. Evan” |
| Noor Aveling (`noor`) | Short operational notes; longer when capacity is being traded away. | “Queue is clear. Keep the restore slot, please. It was the first thing moved last time.” |
| Iris Edran (`kwm034`) | Friendly with colleagues, concise when approving; notices missing handover details. | “Fine for Tuesday, provided Jamie has the previous manifest before you start. Who is carrying the stop decision?” |
| Isabel Nelcourt (`kwm035`) | One specific question, occasional correction; explains a check when useful. | “Which digest did the preview read back? I can see the build digest, but those are two different checks.” |
| Jalen Warden (`kwm036`) | Plain language, clear next action; remembers who covered work. | “Jonas takes the first check. Noor covered the last window; leave them the capacity review this time.” |
| Jamie Elvarn (`kwm037`) | Prefers talking; writes the decision and verifies the finish. | “As discussed: keep the old bundle until the next shift has read the new one. I've put the stop point in the handover.” |
| Jonas Odrin (`kwm038`) | Direct, short; adds context when the recipient needs it. | “Restore sample matches. Three files checked; the whole collection was not restored.” |

Machine notices use stable build, registry, preview, or recovery formats with
actual record values. They have no greeting, personality, or essay about the
meaning of a successful check.
