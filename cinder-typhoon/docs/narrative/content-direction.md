# Direction for later enterprise content

This is an authoring brief. The company profiles, character relationships, and
ordinary stories establish the world; later messages and records will express
it. This pass creates none of those individual assets.

## Write from the employee's point of view

Every record should have a reason to exist for somebody in the company: a
request, an explanation, a completed piece of work, an arrangement, a useful
reference, or an exchange with someone they know. An employee normally writes
to their recipient, not to an observer who needs the whole company explained.

A person's job influences the formality of a record. It does not supply their
entire voice. Clara's engineering note and her holiday recommendations differ;
Evan's release summary and their exchange with Noor differ. The relationship,
audience, and amount of time available matter alongside the sender's habits.

Use shared history economically. Familiar colleagues can refer to something
without repeating its origin. A newcomer may reasonably need more explanation.
Some messages are short because nothing else needs saying. Not every reply
contains a joke, a disagreement, or a significant character moment.

### Prose lint and character voice

The pack's [Vale configuration](../../.vale.ini) exempts authored workplace records,
rendered narrative content, and character profiles with their writing samples
from developer-documentation style rules. Challenge descriptions and hints
are also exempt; the technical and other authoring sections remain checked.
Asset READMEs and the other worldbuilding guides remain authoring documentation.

Review character writing against the person's profile, audience, and situation.
Keep plausible fragments, misspellings, punctuation habits, and differences in
English fluency where they serve that voice. Do not normalize these to satisfy
a prose linter, or add mistakes as a quota. Check chronology, factual consistency,
attachments, and who can read a record independently of its writing style.

Keep lint exclusions in the pack configuration. Do not add lint-control
comments or authoring explanations to assets that participants can inspect.

new requests. Records need not always concern the most prominent customer or
asset. The supporting businesses should have their own reasons for contacting
the company and their own definition of a useful reply.

## Let a mailbox show a position in the social world

The fifteen recurring people do not need equal quantities of mail or identical
surfaces. Rosa's ordinary work produces many brief customer exchanges. Mina's
work leaves shift and training records as well as staff correspondence. Rowan's
work includes repository discussions and personal work notes. Leah receives
summaries that omit detail available to the people doing the work.

Keep recipients and chronology coherent. A sender's and recipient's retained
copies represent the same exchange, but their wider collections differ. Someone
who only receives a final summary should not inexplicably have the preceding
private conversation. A friendship does not make every member of a department
part of it.

Introduce background colleagues where their work or a social exchange requires
them. Give them a coherent function, employer, and voice appropriate to their
brief appearance. They need not all become recurring protagonists. Jules and
supplier staff principally appear through correspondence retained by KeplerOps
or ARWC, with their external employment intact.

## Develop stories without filling every gap

[Workplace life](workplace-life.md) establishes eight ordinary story outlines and
their September states. Later authoring should select enough moments to make a
relationship and its change intelligible. It should not turn every outline into
a long sequence of messages or force a personal exchange into an inappropriate
work record.

The bicycle story may leave a short arrangement and a later thank-you. The
photography event may leave an announcement, results, and a small exchange among
friends. The more private meaning of Evan and Noor's lunch can remain in their
own correspondence. A broad company announcement should not narrate it for them.

Allow different outcomes. Some work is finished. A friendship has adjusted but
retains its differences. A workshop is still planned. A career ambition may
remain undecided for months. People do not all learn a lesson on the same day.

Humour should be selective and affectionate. Theo's effort to appear casual
about a competition is enough; he need not become ridiculous in every appearance.
A routine note can be entirely serious. A person can enjoy their work without
expressing that enjoyment in every message.

## Boundaries for later asset authors

| Authorship | Owns |
| --- | --- |
| World canon | People, history, relationships, business roles, customs, and ordinary story facts in this directory. |
| Ambient content | Correspondence, calendars, directory texture, and ordinary work expressing that canon. |
| Individual challenge technical sections | Exact required clues, secrets, versions, access-bearing records, permissions, and proof. |

Ambient material should be removable without breaking a required route or losing
an essential clue. Do not duplicate protected challenge records into ordinary
mail, or make personal interests and relationships into authentication trivia.
Natural subjects, dates, authors, and completion states should make records
understandable; atmosphere should not become a mass of misleading puzzles.

Opening content uses the retained September snapshot. An intended workshop or
staff event is not already completed. Any later consequence material must reflect
an event that has actually occurred in its owning design. The separate
[campaign continuity document](campaign-story.md) owns those connections.

All workplace stories stay within the editorial tone in the [world overview](README.md).
Personal exchanges are voluntary and respectful. Ordinary disagreement, awkwardness,
ambition, and disappointment are available; criminal conduct and harassment are
outside the workplace fiction. No real person's records or correspondence are
source material for synthetic employees.

Use **keplerops.com** and **alterrawaterco.com** for their respective employers;
the author owns and has authorized both domains. Supporting companies may use
reserved `.test` names until author-controlled domains are assigned, following
[IANA's reservation](https://www.iana.org/assignments/special-use-domain-names).
Reconcile the existing display-name aliases before creating accounts or assets.
This brief leaves message counts, account realization, and implementation for
later work.
