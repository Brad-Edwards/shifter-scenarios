# Campaign operations and outcomes: draft 3

These contracts supply the six new operations funded by the
[allocation audit](allocation-audit.md). Their twenty achievements already
belong to the [portfolio](operation-portfolio.md); they add no bonus flags.
They remain optional. None supplies a hidden prerequisite for ordinary ARWC
entry, OT visibility, or the reservoir ending.

The [player-experience contract](player-experience.md#memorable-consequences-on-the-way-to-the-reservoir)
connects selected outcomes across the campaign into visible conclusions and
callbacks. Those effects use the existing achievements and preserve the routes
below; they are not a new sequence of required challenges.

The named sources establish mechanisms and historical difficulty. Added campaign
constraints are authored adaptations whose assisted difficulty needs testing.
CloudGoat is a published challenge lab rather than a timed competition; its
Hard labels are recorded on that basis. No source's deployment layout is adopted.

## K29: The next release is ours

**Question:** can Cinder retain a precise supplier delivery capability when the
current release credentials stop working?

The support engineer has scheduled a routine release rollover. Completing the
ordinary customer route remains a complete achievement. This optional operation
lets the player own a repeatable delivery mechanism. Its private rehearsal
projects a future release; it never revokes access in the player's main campaign.

Starting evidence comes from G01/G02 plus the K09 job records, K13 cloud records,
or K04's recovered exception queue. These alternatives reveal the same build
dossier. They do not grant its protected capabilities. The source is
[C27: PipeDream](reference-catalog.md#c27), with an authored persistence extension.

| Card | Tier | Distinct work and proof |
| --- | --- | --- |
| K29.1 | M | Cross the build-record authorization boundary to obtain the limited release principal. Prove its actual scope against the dossier service. |
| K29.2 | H | Combine historical source access and signing-material recovery to establish usable release authority. The current checkout alone is insufficient; the recovered material must match the accepted signer. |
| K29.3 | H | Construct one signed release that changes ARWC's behavior while preserving the reference customer's behavior across both current and retained supported interfaces. Independent consumer results must agree with the signed revision and content. |
| K29.4 | X | Abuse the separate permission to change a build definition so a replacement job regenerates the intended artifact after the disclosed old-key withdrawal. Prove a fresh receipt with the old credential rejected and the unrelated customer's behavior unchanged. |

The final step uses a writable build definition and its surviving service
identity. Replaying the old package or storing another copy of the revoked key
cannot satisfy it. These conditions must be visible in the supplied policy and
rollover contract. They are not surprise defenses introduced during play.

**Conclusion:** the support ticket closes on a release Cinder can reproduce.
The player keeps an unscored receipt showing the initial delivery, rejected old
credential, and successful replacement delivery. Finishing K29 never gives
direct OT authority.

K09.4 job execution or K13.4 workload access supplies the authenticated context

the lead, but cannot substitute for that access.
[C28: codebuild_secrets](reference-catalog.md#c28)
supplies the separation between authority over a backup and access to its
source data. The final join also needs G02's customer binding, available through
the ordinary medium route.

| Card | Tier | Distinct work and proof |
| --- | --- | --- |
| K30.1 | M | Recover a different scoped backup principal from protected build metadata. Demonstrate its permitted operation while source-database access still fails. |
| K30.2 | H | Use the backup/restore trust gap to obtain the protected historical records. Creating a copy, gaining access to it, and reading it are one achievement. |
| K30.3 | M | Reconcile a changed customer identifier and migration revision against G02. Recover the correct ARWC migration record from the archive; a plausible record for another tenant fails. |

**Conclusion:** Cinder recovers the missing commissioning history that explains
ARWC's two representations of the plant. It gives provenance for the dispute
and a useful lead, not a substitute for checking today's deployed revision.
Historical evidence stays useful after the gates have opened.

## W33: The expensive hour

**Question:** can the player choose a release that meets a precise economic
objective across changing conditions while respecting the declared physical
envelope?

This is an optional process-planning operation. It becomes discoverable at
OT visibility. Execution requires PROCESS_INTERPRETATION, MODE, CONTROL, and
W29's consequence plan. None of its work blocks W30.

[C30: Terraforming Mars](reference-catalog.md#c30) supplies the challenge form:
construct timed control inputs against a simulated physical trajectory, subject
to explicit resource and state constraints. Water physics replaces orbital
physics; this is a substantial adaptation, not a transplanted solver. The
original 4/5 rating supports a hard candidate, not an elite claim.

Use a small supplied state model, a finite horizon, and a readable forecast.
The outlet has declared ramp limits and operating windows; storage, inflow,
demand, channel capacity, and replacement price vary as documented. The goal
is a bounded loss of usable reserve at a costly point in the allocation cycle.
Holding every gate open must fail the declared constraints. If choosing the
largest static setting solves the task, the hard design has failed.

| Card | Tier | Distinct work and proof |
| --- | --- | --- |
| W33.1 | M | Fit the current calibration and observation delay to a supplied trace and reproduce its forecast within a stated tolerance. A normal commissioning record supplies the inputs; W16 offers an optional alternative. |
| W33.2 | H | Construct a feasible time-varying command schedule in the practice model. Independent evaluation checks every state constraint and the economic objective, not a preferred sequence. |
| W33.3 | H | Exploit a scheduler boundary that validates individual requests but omits the aggregate allocation check. Translate the plan into accepted, correctly scoped live actions under the current mode, and demonstrate the resulting bounded loss. |
| W33.4 | X | Construct a feedback policy that succeeds across disclosed demand/calibration cases using observations available to the controller. Run it through the earned scheduler capability in private live rehearsals drawn from the published bounds. Replaying one winning schedule is insufficient. |

The scheduler flaw is an authored addition. Its scope must match the earned
control capability; the plan does not create authority. Feedback cases test
generalization, not hidden parameters or an unexplained scoring formula.
Simulation advances on demand and needs ordinary CPU computation, not hours
of wall-clock waiting or expensive search.

**Conclusion:** a timeline shows when the release caused replacement purchases
and restrictions, and which constraints the operator had to manage. Physical
effects remain inside the same non-catastrophic fiction as W30. A specialist
has a process problem worth discussing rather than another register to write.

## W34: The reassuring dashboard

**Question:** can Cinder make a planning decision rely on a false operational
view while the actual reservoir follows a different trajectory?

W34.1 and W34.2 require PROCESS_INTERPRETATION plus either W23's estimator
corruption or W27's accepted diagnostic program. They investigate and influence
the planning consumer before live control is needed. W34.3 adds CONTROL, MODE,
and CONSEQUENCE_PLAN for its live release. Those alternative compromises act on
the same declared planning-view interface. Neither grants ownership of raw
telemetry or the independent evaluator. Their exploits receive credit in their
original operations and are not scored again here.

[C13](reference-catalog.md#c13) and [C15](reference-catalog.md#c15) supply the
underlying demanding mechanisms. The following reporting/decision composition
is new work. Difficulty is conditional on those mechanisms already being earned.

| Card | Tier | Distinct work and proof |
| --- | --- | --- |
| W34.1 | M | Identify the planning consumer and its actual inputs, refresh interval, and validity rules. Prove a harmless changed estimate reaches that consumer without changing raw measurements. |
| W34.2 | H | Construct a false planning view that passes the declared consistency checks against unaffected meters and known delays, causing the planner to select a different allocation. A green screen alone is insufficient. |
| W34.3 | X | Couple the view to a bounded release through a disclosed reporting/shift transition. Prove the changed planning decision, actual reserve loss, and continued consistency with every unaffected observation over the specified interval. |

**Conclusion:** the planner issues a reassuring report while independent
observations show the real volume loss and a comparison identifies the changed
allocation decision. The controls engineer's earlier warning can return if the
player recovered it; the earned planning and process evidence explains the
discrepancy on its own. This is an optional deeper ending with the same bounded
consequences, not a larger disaster.

The player starts a private rehearsal from a chosen earned checkpoint. Prior
scores and the main campaign's gate result persist. Scenario time can advance
immediately. Both reporting and physical effects are graded; editing the judge's
state or merely replaying W30 earns no new credit.

before it controls the plant?

Available from corporate ARWC execution. This is an original easy/medium
application-and-data episode. Procurement exists because the company buys
replacement supply; its records have a purpose beyond hiding credentials.

| Card | Tier | Distinct work and proof |
| --- | --- | --- |
| W35.1 | E | Recover the exposed replacement-supply price catalog, a surface separate from W09's allocation ledger. |
| W35.2 | M | Cross the quote object's authorization boundary to recover the restricted district quote and its binding identifiers. |
| W35.3 | M | Exploit the importer's confusion between report district and purchasing district to issue an unauthorized order against the wrong budget. An independent order receipt proves the limited simulated liability. |

**Conclusion:** the maintenance planner receives an unexpected purchase order.
The player sees the order and the liability against the wrong district, which
procurement routinely thanks for supporting regional resilience. The dry joke
is authored acknowledgement after the verified order, never another acceptance
condition. The handler acknowledges a completed financial objective. Nothing
moves in the plant, and no real purchasing or payment service participates.
This is a complete local ending for a participant who does not reach OT.

## Source and acceptance boundaries

The new operations expand the kinds of demanding work. They do not establish
a measured nation-state-operator ceiling. K29.4, W33.3/W33.4, and W34's joins
particularly need fresh source-aware assisted solves because their added
constraints determine their difficulty.

Require substantive remaining work after prerequisites. A capstone fails if
its apparent depth comes entirely from redoing an already scored exploit,
collecting completion markers, adding API calls, or waiting for a simulated
shift. Keep their consequences even if calibration merges or lowers a card.
