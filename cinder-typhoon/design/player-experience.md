# Player experience and event rhythm: draft 3

This describes the choices and feedback the architecture must support. It does
not prescribe screen layouts, topology, a specific agent product, or final
dialogue. Players remain individuals; the provided agent is their intended
capability uplift, and full delegation is allowed.

## How choices become legible

Show a small current lead set with an expandable list of other discovered work.
Do not hide earned choices to force an intended sequence. Each lead gives:
the operational question, the capability already held, the kind of work,
the proposed difficulty, and the consequence. Mark missing evidence honestly.
A source file visible early does not imply permission to use its live service.

Use three scope labels: **short success**, **investigation**, and **research**.
They describe the shape of work until assisted timings exist. They are not
minute estimates. Provide a recommended next lead with a reason, while letting
the participant or agent choose another. Keep completed local objectives visible
alongside unfinished ones; the board should not imply failure for leaving work.

### After the reservoir objective

The gate result receives its conclusion immediately. The handler then offers
optional follow-on objectives: hold supplier delivery through a release change
(K29), reconstruct the customer migration (K30), choose a more precise economic
effect (W33), or manipulate the planning view that accompanies a release (W34).
Each states its remaining prerequisites; completing W30 does not grant every
optional privilege. Other discovered work remains selectable.

These operations are available as soon as their actual prerequisites are earned,
including before W30 where applicable. They are not a new compulsory phase or
content withheld for day two. A participant who finishes early has meaningful
choices; a participant who never finishes still has completed local episodes.

## A concrete co-hacking episode: water on paper

The player has reached corporate ARWC and chooses the missing-reserve lead.
Their objective is to explain why the allocation report and meter export
disagree. This uses existing W09 achievements, with no extra score for interaction.

1. **An understandable objective.** The lead shows the support ticket's claim
   that the report is healthy and links the actual available records. The player
   can delegate the whole investigation or ask to work through it with the agent.
2. **A proposed investigation.** The agent identifies the report period, unit
   definitions, and source revisions it needs. Its short status names evidence
   already obtained and an assumption still being tested.
3. **A recoverable wrong turn.** If the agent sums old and current asset aliases
   as separate equipment, a reconciliation check exposes duplicate physical
   identifiers. This is an authentic record-migration issue, documented in the
   artifacts, not an injected agent failure or a required mistake. A correct
   first attempt continues immediately.
4. **A meaningful choice.** The participant or delegated agent can reconcile
   the identifier history, inspect a sample meter interval, or park the issue
   and follow the maintenance appointment. These choices remain useful without
   requiring the player to invent tool syntax.
5. **A visible result.** A comparison shows the original total, corrected
   interpretation, and specific records responsible. The handler acknowledges
   an intelligence result: Cinder now understands which reserve the company is
   relying on. Raw evidence remains one click or agent query away.
6. **A natural continuation.** The next leads offer the read integration,
   replacement procurement, and the deeper archived-history investigation.
   Stopping here leaves a complete explanation and a retained evidence packet.

The interface must distinguish observed facts from hypotheses and show what
changed. It need not ask the human to approve every command or explain the
solution to earn credit. Agency can consist of selecting a purpose and observing
its accomplishment while delegating the mechanics.

Training teaches this same rhythm: choose a bounded objective, let the agent
inspect and act, check independent evidence, and redirect when a hypothesis
fails. Optional explanations and basic tool guidance are free. A novice's first
success must not depend on knowing how to prompt a coding agent professionally.

## Sixteen hours of optional depth

The event has approximately sixteen competitive hours across two days. There
are no global phase unlocks. Playtesting must observe complete individual
trajectories, rather than adding unrelated per-card medians.

| Experience interval | Design obligation | Failure signal |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Opening session | Offer a meaningful first effect within the existing ten-minute test target after setup; training remains optional. | Setup or choosing a task consumes the session, or the participant cannot identify any accomplishment. |
| Sustained day-one play | Alternate short successes, investigations, and optional research at earned positions. Let a stalled player preserve useful progress and change activity. | The only available worthwhile work depends on the same blocked specialty. |
| Before a break | Preserve evidence, credentials, partial proof, last failed hypothesis, and a next useful action. | Returning requires reconstructing a long transcript or redoing scored work. |
| Day-two return | Show the player's chosen unfinished objective, why it matters, and two alternatives already within reach. | The participant has access but cannot remember a reason to continue. |
| Late play | Keep substantive unresolved depth for fast specialists and satisfying local conclusions for others. | Remaining content is mostly repeated collection, or a specialist has exhausted worthwhile work long before the window ends. |

The ten-minute target and the intended depth are hypotheses to test, not
guarantees. If additional capacity is necessary, add distinct work in the
specialty where the ceiling failed. Increasing the number of flags on one exploit
does not repair a shortage of content.

## Recovery and bounded rehearsals

K29, W33, and W34 change scenario state during private rehearsals. Starting one
shows the checkpoint, the declared transition, and what proof it will evaluate.
It preserves earned capabilities and main-campaign results. Resetting a failed
rehearsal resets its local effects, not other operations or another player.

Simulation time advances explicitly and quickly. Pausing for a talk or stopping
overnight does not miss a maintenance window. Competitive execution pauses
outside the published event hours; unattended agents receive no overnight
advantage. Day-two summaries are generated from retained evidence, with uncertain
claims marked, rather than relying on the agent's memory alone.

## A shared event with individual progress

Use a brief optional opening dispatch, an optional day-two recap, and a closing
debrief. Make their text available asynchronously. They supply purpose and
recognize accomplishments without withholding technical clues or stopping play.

The event display can celebrate varied milestones: first independent effect,
customer accepted, missing reserve explained, first live trace, durable supplier
access, and an advanced investigation completed. Recognitions are unscored;
repeat actions do not add points. Personal receipts retain the player's own
revision, evidence, or consequence so a newcomer has something specific to show.

The room's progress display summarizes exercise accomplishments across individual
ranges. It must not claim that all players jointly changed one physical plant.
No first solver opens another player's route. The main scoreboard remains an
individual competition, with campaign milestones shown separately.

Permit discussion of techniques, documentation, and agent troubleshooting.
Prohibit sharing live flags, player credentials, and current-instance solutions
between participants. Public pre-existing writeups remain allowed under the
challenge-reuse policy. Published hints are available on the same terms to all
players; support staff distinguish infrastructure help from solution hints.
People can help each other learn while each participant earns their own effects.

The closing debrief explains selected cross-organization callbacks and celebrates
several kinds of achievement. It must make sense to someone who stayed inside
KeplerOps. A complete personal operation, rather than universal campaign
completion, is the unit of success the event should make memorable.
