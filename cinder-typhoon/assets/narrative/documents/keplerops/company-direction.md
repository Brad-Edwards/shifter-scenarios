# A specialist company with room to grow

Leah Calder-Voss • Company planning note • 2 September 2026

KeplerOps is profitable. We have a working cash reserve and intend to fund the
next stage of growth from retained earnings and work we have contracted to do.
We remain privately held and founder-controlled, with minority ownership among
some early employees. There is no investor deadline requiring us to become a
different size by a particular year.

Our roughly twelve utility customers buy substantial organizational services.
Subscriptions and ongoing support provide most revenue. Implementation,
migrations, and scoped integration work provide the remainder. Several customer
contracts cover more than one department or site. Renewals, procurement exercises,
and expansion within customers are a substantial part of the commercial team's
work.

Over the next two years, I want us to add a few suitable customers and reduce
the share of engineering that must be repeated for each one. That requires
choices. A bespoke project can pay for useful capacity while consuming the
people who would otherwise make the product easier to maintain.

We should consider proposals against three questions:

- Does the work suit the product we intend to support?
- Can we meet the commitment while keeping our existing commitments?
- What will remain in our ordinary workload after the project closes?

The last question includes maintenance, support, documentation, and the work
an experienced person keeps doing after moving to a different role. Please
bring examples to your manager. An improvement need not be a new feature to
be worth funding.

We have 64 colleagues now. I would like this to remain somewhere people can
build a long career, including those who want to remain specialists. A good
next year should make the company more durable as well as larger.
